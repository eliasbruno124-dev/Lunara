# Partner tip quality

## Brief context and a suitable offer

All 71 German and English cards were revised for 1.2.1.
The standard is brief, understandable context plus a useful idea,
such as a concrete offer, a small way to ease everyday life, or time together.
The copy should sound like a partnership, not a lecture.
Boundaries are stated where needed, rather than repeating the same
prohibitions on every card.

Phase cards 51–59 and 66–71, available through `sharePhase` or
`shareFertileWindow`, each include a brief explanatory fact about the cycle.
Cards with a specific symptom context respond only to explicitly shared symptoms.
General cards require no health data. A picnic or movie night is an original
date idea, not a recommendation derived from hormones.
No phase establishes mood, performance, or sexual wishes.

Only the public descriptions of
[Flo for Partners](https://flo.health/product-tour/flo-for-partners) and
[Flo stories](https://help.flo.health/hc/en-us/articles/360061400591-What-are-Flo-stories)
inspired the format, with short pieces of information alongside practical
ideas. Paid Premium content was neither accessed nor copied. Lunara's copy
is original. No equivalence to Flo Premium is claimed.

## The person takes priority over the catalog

Under **Settings → What really helps me**, the tracker can explicitly
share personal wishes with their partner account and disable them again.
These wishes take priority in selection and are displayed verbatim,
without automatic translation. Private notes are not used for this purpose.
Partner cards do not link to the editing view that exists only in the
tracker account.

Offers can be declined or skipped. Previously agreed personal or shared
responsibilities do not require a new task assignment.
Touch, personal belongings, spending, and changes to shared plans follow
your agreements. Liking a suggestion or saving a preference does not
constitute consent at the present moment.

## Selection and verifiable boundaries

The 71 stable IDs are retained, together with feedback and dismissals.
“Another suggestion” only browses the cards. It does not record negative feedback.
Without context, the personal catalog rotates daily without repetition
until the available pool is exhausted. Categories are interleaved within
pools. A limited selection can still produce consecutive cards from the same category.

After personal wishes, shared phase/symptom context is used on two of every
three days when general ideas are also available.
Broad preferences such as household help do not displace this context.
Without such context, explicitly matching preferences or helpful feedback
receive those two slots. The third day is reserved for a general idea.
Other eligible suggestions remain available when browsing.
If only one pool is available, selection uses that pool.
Changes to sharing or wishes can change the order.

Built-in cards have explicit internal action mappings. Warmth is not touch,
and a reminder for one's own calendar is not a reminder to the tracker.
A preference switch that is off still means unknown rather than rejected.
Explicitly unwanted terms are matched against the call to action and its
mapped action labels. This normalized text matching does not understand
arbitrary synonyms or negations. A copy change therefore also requires
reviewing the mapping.

health-related statements, not the date ideas or the catalog's effectiveness.

| [Office on Women’s Health – Your menstrual cycle](https://womenshealth.gov/menstrual-cycle/your-menstrual-cycle) | 36, 53, 55, 56, 59, 67 |

The wording consists of short paraphrases, not copied specialist texts.
Links belong only to their mapped global cards, never to personal wishes
or private inputs. The browser opens only after a tap. A source link
provides neither individual advice nor evidence of a medical review of
the product.

Both warmth suggestions retain the person's wishes, manufacturer guidance,
a cloth, and a personal temperature check. Guidance on seeking medical
assessment is general information, not a claim about symptoms that are
currently unshared. Lunara does not confirm ovulation and is not a method
of contraception.

The catalog does not recommend medication or replace medical assessment.
It has not been reviewed by a medical expert panel and promises no individual
benefit. Automated tests check completeness, length, known problematic
wording, source mapping, permissions, selection, and concrete counterexamples.
They measure neither empathy nor medical quality.

There is no generative runtime feature, and no health data is sent to such
a service. Copy and selection remain on the user's own server.
